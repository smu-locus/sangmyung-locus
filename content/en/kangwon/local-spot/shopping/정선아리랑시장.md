The Jeongseon 5-Day Market is held on days ending in 2 or 7, and it is filled with local residents as well as travelers from all over the country. But it wasn’t always this crowded. In the past, this was a humble rural market where local residents bought and sold wild greens, agricultural products, and daily necessities. Later, as Jeongseon's coal industry flourished, the market also became very active, but it went through a period of stagnation as the industry declined. Then, in 1999, with the launch of the Jeongseon Five-Day Market tourist train (now the Jeongseon Arirang Train A-Train), it regained its vitality and took on the form it has today.
At the market, you can taste Jeongseon’s distinctive foods, such as gondre rice with its sharp aroma, chewy pan-fried millet rice cake, and the delicious ‘nodung-chigi’—a slurping technique that makes you slurp until your nose is coated. It is also perfect for grocery shopping, filled with seasonal specialties such as gomchwi and chamnamul in spring, glutinous corn in summer, and wild grapes in autumn. Especially if you visit during the five-day market or on a weekend (Saturday), you can experience a more vibrant market atmosphere. In addition, the permanent artisan Jeongseon Arirang Market is also in operation, so no matter when you visit, you’ll feel the warm atmosphere of Jeongseon’s traditional market.
How about strolling through Jeongseon Arirang Market, filled with local cuisine and seasonal specialties, and discovering the unique charm of Jeongseon?
