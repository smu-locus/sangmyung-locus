Cheongpyeongsa is the head temple of Sinheungsa in the 3rd district of the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism and a representative temple of Chuncheon. It was founded in 973, the 24th year of King Gwangjong of Goryeo, underwent three reconstructions, and was rebuilt by the monk Bowu during the reign of King Myeongjong of Joseon, becoming a grand temple. After being almost destroyed during the Korean War, the pavilions were rebuilt in the 1970s, the revolving lantern was restored, and the bell pavilion and monks’ quarters were relocated. Additionally, Cheongpyeongsa’s Goryeo Seonwon is the oldest Goryeo garden site, predating the garden designed by Saihō of Kyoto, Japan, by 200 years. Today, Cheongpyeongsa consists of buildings such as the Daeungjeon Hall, Geungnakbojeon Hall, Samseonggak Shrine, the Treasure Hall, Gwaneumjeon Hall, Nahanjeon Hall, and the monks’ quarters, and can be visited by boat from Soyanggang Dam, earning it the nickname 'the temple in the island.'
