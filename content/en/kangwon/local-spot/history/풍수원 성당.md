In the early 1800s, Pungsu-won Cathedral was a refuge where about 40 believers, led by Shin Tae-bo (Peter), settled in Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, to escape persecution. Pungsuwon Parish once oversaw 29 chapels across 12 counties, including Chuncheon, Wonju, Hwacheon, Yanggu, Hongcheon, Hoengseong, Pyeongchang, and Yangpyeong; starting with the separation of Wonju Parish in 1896, Chuncheon Parish was separated in 1920, and Hongcheon Parish in 1948.
The main church building, situated to the southwest on a low hill, features a three-bay plan with a column arcade and a ceiling structure that separates the nave from the transept. The main entrance protruding at the center front, the circular windows on the second floor, and the bell tower’s two-arched windows and two-tiered finial windows on the third floor emphasize verticality with walls that narrow toward the top, making the central facade striking. 
How about pausing your walk for a moment and spending a reverent, peaceful time at Pungsuwon Cathedral, where the long history of Catholicism blends with its historic architecture?
