The Yanggu Prehistoric and Modern History Museum is Korea’s first museum dedicated to the prehistoric era, where you can experience and observe prehistoric life firsthand. The museum’s exterior reflects prehistoric pit dwellings, and with five exhibition halls and two outdoor dolmen parks, you can view artifacts from different periods.
This museum exhibits 654 Paleolithic, Neolithic, and Bronze Age artifacts from the Yanggu region, focusing on Paleolithic artifacts from Sangmuryong-ri, Yanggu-eup, and Neolithic and Bronze Age artifacts from Hyeon-ri, Haean-myeon. In addition, prehistoric artifacts from the Yanggu region, which were unearthed in large quantities in areas such as Sangmuryong in Yanggu-eup following the draining of Paroho Lake for the construction of the Peace Dam in 1987, are currently displayed across three museums: the National Museum of Korea, Kangwon National University Museum, and Kyung Hee University Museum. Out of more than 500 artifacts, half are from the Paleolithic era, while about 50 each are from the Neolithic and Bronze Ages. Additionally, a touchscreen is installed in the lobby, allowing anyone to easily tour the exhibition halls and receive guidance. 
In a space where history and culture come alive, the Yanggu Prehistoric and Modern History Museum invites you to follow the footsteps of precious cultural heritage and experience a profound journey where past and present connect!
