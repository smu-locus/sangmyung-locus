Located on the slopes of Obongsan, one of the three great mountains of Gwandong along with Geumgangsan and Seoraksan, this place derives its name from Botanakgasan, said to be the place where Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva always dwells while preaching; throughout history, pilgrims have continuously made earnest prayers to meet the deity of Gwaneum, regardless of their status or rank. Naksansa is a place of stunning scenery overlooking the East Sea, famous for its 16-meter-tall statue of Haesugwaneum, Uisangdae—a pavilion built atop a coastal cliff and a renowned East Coast sunrise spot—Hongryeonam Hermitage overlooking the sea, the seven-story stone pagoda of Naksansa in Yangyang built in the Joseon period but inheriting Goryeo architectural style (a treasure), and the Haesugwaneum Gongsu Sarira Pagoda, stele, and relics of the sarira complex in Yangyang where Buddha’s sacred relics were discovered. In 2005, a major wildfire destroyed more than 20 halls, including the designated treasure one, but they have since all been restored to their former glory. 
How about walking the 'Path to Fulfilling Dreams' at Naksansa, the beautiful temple of the East Sea that has withstood countless years, placing a small stone on a stone pagoda and making your own wish?
