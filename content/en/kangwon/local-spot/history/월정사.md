Woljeongsa Temple was founded in 643, the 12th year of Queen Seondeok of Silla, by the monk Jajang on Odaesan, a mountain that is a sacred Buddhist site. As the head temple of the 4th district of the Jogye Order, it oversees about 60 temples and 8 hermitages, and is home to numerous cultural treasures, including the National Treasure octagonal nine-story stone pagoda, the Woljeongsa Stone Seated Bodhisattva, and the wooden seated Manjusri statue. Also, along the approximately 1 km stretch from Woljeongsa’s Iljumun Gate to Geumganggyo Bridge, there is a fir forest with about 1,700 trees. This is one of Woljeongsa’s signature tourist attractions and is loved by visitors in every season, so it is a must-see if you visit the temple. 
Woljeongsa offers temple stays in two formats—restorative and experiential—where you can leave the secular world and recharge your body and mind in the mountains. In addition to temple stays, there is Woljeongsa’s monastic school for the general public to experience the life of a monk. Additionally, Odaesan Meditation Village, abbreviated as OMV, spans approximately 99,170 square meters—equivalent to 14 soccer fields—and offers accommodations, cultural experience facilities, restaurants, gardens, and forest trails, providing those worn out by work and competition with a chance to restore their mind and body through meditation in a natural sanctuary. In addition, they run a meditation yoga school for modern people worn out by life.
At Woljeongsa, where deep history and the tranquility of nature blend, soothe your weary mind and recharge your spirit!
